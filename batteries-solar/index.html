Batteries & solarv1.2

One cell only gives a small voltage and can only supply so much. To get more, cells are wired together:

  • Series: end to end, + to −. The voltages add. The current and amp-hours stay the same as one cell.

  • Parallel: side by side, + to + and − to −. The voltage stays the same as one row. The current and amp-hours add.

  • Series-parallel: rows of cells in series, with the rows wired in parallel: more voltage and more amp-hours. Sometimes written like 3S2P: 3 in series, 2 in parallel.

In the symbols above, the long line of each cell is + and the short line is −.

Primary cells are used once and thrown away (recycled). They can’t be recharged: trying can make them leak or burst.

Cell

A solar cell turns light into electricity. Each silicon cell makes about 0.5 V no matter its size; more light (or a bigger cell) gives more current.
